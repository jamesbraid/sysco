# Capacity limits of the L1 module table, the L2 configuration, the power lists and the partition database

Re-reads the table "What changes for 128 processors" in [l1-conversation.md](l1-conversation.md) (section 12 of that note) against the shipped bytes and adds what an L2 does when an L1 reports more records than the L1 firmware can hold. Written 2026-11-13. L2 facts are from L2 1.44.0 (`l2.bin` of IRIX 6.5.30, sha256 `6bcfda07a24cb4d56850922dab17a98223abcd14221d307e3a8c3778434e8761`; `bin/l2` sha256 `9923b1ee573c2815...`, `lib/libmain` and `lib/libpart` carved as in [image-layout.md](image-layout.md); executables load at `0x10000000`, libraries at 0). L1 facts are from L1 1.44.0 (sha256 `c355c542554a41b23b69dca4c201c566e8352e8f9fb870d2365c74cdd8ab0c0f`), packages 0, 1 and 2 individually; the L1 half has its own note, [../l1/module-table-capacity.md](../l1/module-table-capacity.md). Grades: established, inferred, unknown, as in [../../AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md). Every row of the earlier table is marked "re-read: confirmed", "re-read: corrected" or "not re-read".

Neither controller has a field indexed by processors. The units that scale are bricks, racks and ports, and the limits below are in those units. No brick count for a 128-processor system is read from any image or used here.

## The correction that matters most

**The L1 module table has 10 records, not 15.** A host that walks 15 entries walks five that do not exist in L1 packages 0, 1 and 2 (the Origin 3000 C-brick packages are 0 and 2). The capacity word is the immediate 10 in all three; package 3 and 5 choose 10, 45, 85 or 125 by brick type. The firmware's own position space is 15 wide (0; 1 to 4; 5 to 9; 10 to 14) but the allocation covers 0 to 9, and a brick type with two node ports writes its second group of five past the table. A C-brick opens one node port, so it uses positions 0 to 9 only. Details, the exact behaviour at position 10 and the per-package addresses are in [../l1/module-table-capacity.md](../l1/module-table-capacity.md). Established.

## What the L2 does if an L1 reports more than 10 records

An L1 with the shipped packages 0, 1 or 2 cannot (rows 1 and 14), so this is about a peer that is not one of them: the L2 puts no cap anywhere on the path.

1. **Parse** (row 4). Each record is copied into a growing array of 0x30-byte brick records; N is taken from the packet. Nothing overflows in the L2 for any N the packet really carries: the array grows by `(count + 1) x 0x30` each step. The failure modes are an unchecked `realloc` return (a null return makes the next store go to address `i x 0x30`) and a stated N larger than the data actually sent (the loop reads index bytes and records past the descriptor blocks).
2. **Change test.** After parsing, the old and new arrays are compared (count, then `memcmp` of `count x 0x30`) and a difference sets the "table changed" result that re-arms the query timer at 1 s ([config-request-reply.md](config-request-reply.md)). No size dependence.
3. **Routing.** `FindBrickIP` walks every record of every port in state 2 with no early limit; the stored index byte decides ties (smallest wins). No fixed-size copy of the array exists on this path.
4. **Brick selection for the power and reset commands** (row 9). The only fixed-size structure fed from the arrays is the 1024-word list in the destination context. It is filled from `ports x records`, de-duplicated by brick address, and unbounded: overflow needs 1025 distinct addresses, more than 40 ports x 10 records.
5. **Console selection** (`L2CfgMGetConsole`) keeps two candidates per partition in fixed arrays indexed by partition number; the partition byte comes from the record. Partition numbers of 64 or more index beyond the arrays the note [two-partition-nvram-and-console.md](two-partition-nvram-and-console.md) describes; whether the array is large enough was not measured.

So in the L2 the first thing that fails with a larger L1 table is not a 10 or 15 limit but the 40-port count and, far beyond it, the 1024-word brick list. Established for items 1 to 4; item 5 inferred.

## Rack ranges, stated together

| Use | Range | Why | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 setting and the router word | 0 to 1023 | 10-bit field (bits 27 to 18 of the word) | earlier grade (settings note) |
| L2 routing and power commands | 1 to 255 | brick record keeps the low byte; selection skips rack 0 and above 0xff | established |
| Partition tables (brick id, rack-wide layouts, the `p` prefix mask) | 1 to 63 for racks; partition numbers 0 to 63 | 6-bit rack field `0x0fc0`; 64-byte partition mask | established |
| Slots per rack | 1 to 63 in selection; 1 to 39 accepted by the L1 console validator; power-up lists reach 51 | `0x3f` compare; validator `0x26`; lists | established |

A rack of 256 to 1023 is representable in the L1 and in the router word but the L2 never matches it (the stored byte is truncated and the route compare is on the byte), so a system whose racks are numbered above 255 is unroutable by this L2 (inferred from the byte store; the route compare itself was not re-read here).
